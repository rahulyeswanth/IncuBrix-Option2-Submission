# Hooks, captions and the first two seconds

A hook is a promise made in two seconds. If the promise is vague, the viewer leaves. If the promise is specific, the viewer stays to see it kept.

Write the hook before you edit the video. If you cannot say the promise in one sentence, the clip is not ready. A clip without a promise is just footage.

Use numbers when they are real. "Three mistakes in my intro" is clearer than "intro mistakes". A number tells people how long the idea will take.

Never promise something the clip does not deliver. A hook that overpromises gets a click and then a drop. Viewers remember that feeling and scroll past you next time.

Lead with the problem your viewer already has. People pay attention to their own frustrations first. Your solution only matters after they feel understood.

Show the result before the method. If you are teaching an edit, start with the finished clip. Then show how you got there.

Cut the first breath. Most recordings start with a pause, a cough or a half sentence. Trim to the first word that matters.

Put the main words on screen in the first second. A short line of text lets people understand the clip before the audio makes sense. This is especially useful on a noisy commute.

Keep captions to two short lines. Long captions cover the face and hide the point. If a sentence does not fit, split it.

Highlight the word being spoken. Moving emphasis keeps the eye on the screen. It also hides the small gaps between words.

Place captions inside the safe area of the screen. Buttons and descriptions cover the bottom of vertical video. Keep the text well above them.

Read your caption out loud before you post. If you stumble, your viewer will too. Remove any word you would not say to a friend.

Use plain words instead of jargon. Write "cut" rather than "non-linear trim". The simpler word is almost always the better one.

Start the written caption with the benefit. The first line shows before the "more" button, so it must work alone. Details can wait for the second line.

Use few hashtags and make them relevant. Three well chosen tags beat fifteen random ones. Tags help a platform understand the topic, nothing more.

Ask a question people can answer in a few words. "Which tip will you try first?" is easy to answer. "Tell me everything about your process" is not.

Test one change at a time. If you change the hook and the thumbnail together, you will not know which one worked. One variable per test gives you a lesson you can reuse.

Compare posts of the same format only. Compare a short video with a short video and a carousel with a carousel. Mixed comparisons teach you nothing.

Keep a list of hooks that worked. Review it before every recording session. Your own history is the best template you will ever find.

Rewrite a weak hook three times before you give up on the clip. The first version is usually a description. The third version is usually a promise.

End the clip when the point is made. Do not add a long goodbye. A clean ending makes people more likely to watch the next one.
